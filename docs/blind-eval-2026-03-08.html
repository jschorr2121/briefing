Methodology

I approached this as a cold user: no knowledge of the product, its stack, or goals. I explored the deployed app at briefing-five.vercel.app, reviewed the source code for anything the JS-rendered UI couldn't show me via fetch, and examined every user-facing feature, page, and flow. I devised 10 evaluation criteria that I believe capture what makes a news briefing product worth using.

Evaluation Criteria & Scores

1. First Impression & Accessibility 3/10

The custom domain is broken (NXDOMAIN). Users hitting briefing.jakeschorr.com see nothing. The Vercel URL (briefing-five.vercel.app) works but is not memorable or brandable. Once you do reach the app, you're immediately hit with a Google OAuth login wall — no way to preview the product, see a demo, or understand the value before committing credentials. For a product with zero brand recognition, this is a conversion killer. No landing page, no screenshots, no social proof.

2. Onboarding Flow 8/10

Once past auth, the onboarding is genuinely well-done. The 3-step flow (welcome → topic picker → auto-generate) is clean and intuitive. 42 curated topics organized by category with emoji headers, plus custom topic input. Progress dots, smooth animations, and auto-generating the first briefing after selection is a smart move — reduces friction to first value. The only miss: no explanation of what a "briefing" actually looks like before you commit to topics.

3. Core Value Delivery (Briefing Quality) 7/10

The briefing generation pipeline is solid: Perigon API for structured news data + GPT-5-nano for synthesis + semantic search for niche topics. The story card format with headlines, bullet points, sources, and dates is excellent for scanability. Each briefing card shows reading time, story count, and search provider — nice transparency. Summaries support bold formatting. The horizontal story carousel is a clever UI pattern. However, the quality depends entirely on the AI model and Perigon data — there's no editorial layer, no fact-checking indicator, no confidence scoring. Users must trust the output blindly.

4. Design & Visual Polish 8/10

Clean, modern design. The light theme with blue accent (#2563eb) is professional and readable. Custom CSS variables make theming consistent. Good use of cards, rounded corners, subtle gradients, and micro-animations (fadeIn, shimmer loading states). The loading skeleton is particularly well-crafted — it mirrors the actual card layout. Safe area insets for notched devices show mobile awareness. However: no dark mode toggle (the CSS has dark-theme variables but no user control visible), and the userScalable: false viewport setting is an accessibility anti-pattern that prevents pinch-to-zoom.

5. Feature Richness 8/10

Impressive feature set for what appears to be a solo-dev product: topic selection (42 curated + custom), on-demand generation, TTS with 12 voice options and speed control, email delivery, markdown export, shareable links, briefing history (last 10), scheduled daily emails, keyboard shortcuts, copy/share per story, and a full pricing/subscription system. This is genuinely feature-complete. The voice picker popup is a nice touch. Missing: search/filter within briefings, bookmarking individual stories, and reading preferences beyond length/tone.

6. Trust & Source Transparency 7/10

Every story card shows its source and links to the original article — this is essential and well-executed. The "via perigon" badge on briefing cards adds transparency about data provenance. Source pills at the bottom of each section are clickable. However, there's no indication of how many sources were consulted, no diversity-of-perspective indicator, no "last updated" timestamp on individual stories, and no way to verify the AI didn't hallucinate details. For a news product, trust is everything — more signals would help.

7. Mobile Experience 7/10

The app is responsive with mobile-aware CSS (safe-area insets, snap scrolling, swipe hints on story cards, responsive grid breakpoints). The story carousel with "Swipe for more stories" hint shows mobile-first thinking. There's even a native iOS app (SwiftUI) and Android shell (Capacitor). However, the horizontal scroll cards can be finicky on mobile, and the button-heavy action bar (Generate, Schedule, Settings, Listen, Email, Export, Share) could overwhelm on small screens. The userScalable: false is hostile to accessibility users.

8. Monetization & Pricing 6/10

Clear freemium model: 3 daily briefings free, Pro at $6.99/mo or $49.99/yr. Feature gating makes sense (unlimited topics, audio TTS, scheduled delivery for Pro). The pricing page is well-designed with a billing toggle and FAQ. However: Stripe isn't configured on the production deployment (checkout shows "Coming soon"), which means there's no actual way to pay. The upgrade modal and usage counter work, but lead to a dead end. This creates a frustrating bait-and-switch experience for users who want to upgrade.

9. Retention & Habit Formation 5/10

The scheduled email feature is the strongest retention mechanism — if it works. Daily emails create habit loops. Briefing history lets users revisit past briefings. But there's no push notification system (for web), no "streak" or engagement gamification, no personalization that improves over time, no "briefing digest" showing what you missed. The app is entirely pull-based (you come to it) with a single push mechanism (scheduled email). For a daily news product, this isn't enough. Users will forget it exists within a week unless the emails are exceptional.

10. Differentiation & Market Position 6/10

The core differentiator — user-defined topics instead of algorithmic curation — is genuinely valuable and clearly articulated. No other app lets you pick from 42 curated topics + custom topics and generate on-demand briefings. The combination of on-demand + scheduled + audio + export is unique. However: the "AI news summary" space is increasingly crowded (Particle, Concise, Yahoo News Digest, Arc Search, Perplexity Discover). The app lacks editorial voice, community features, or any network effect that would create a moat. It's a utility, not a destination.

Top 5 Recommendations (Priority Order)

1. Fix the DNS (5 minutes, massive impact)

Add a CNAME record for briefing.jakeschorr.com → cname.vercel-dns.com and add the domain in Vercel project settings. Without this, the product literally doesn't exist for anyone you give the URL to.

2. Build a pre-auth landing page (1-2 days)

Show what the product does before requiring Google sign-in. Include: a demo briefing, screenshots, value proposition, and social proof. The current OAuth wall kills 90%+ of potential users. Consider letting people generate one briefing without signing in.

3. Configure Stripe on production (30 minutes)

Set the Stripe env vars on Vercel. Until users can pay, there's no business. The pricing page exists, the checkout flow is built — it just needs env vars.

4. Add a "how fresh is this?" signal (few hours)

Show when articles were published (not just "Today" or "Yesterday" — show hours). For a news product, freshness is credibility. Also consider showing how many sources were scanned to produce each briefing.

5. Web push notifications for scheduled briefings (1-2 days)

Email is good but easy to ignore. Service worker + push notifications would dramatically improve retention. "Your morning briefing is ready" at 7am creates a daily habit.

The Blunt Assessment

Briefing is a well-built product with a clear value proposition that is not ready for distribution. The engineering is solid — the code is clean, the features are thoughtful, the UX flows are polished. But the go-to-market fundamentals are broken: the custom domain doesn't resolve, payments don't work, and there's no way to evaluate the product without committing to Google OAuth. These aren't hard problems to fix — they're configuration tasks, not engineering challenges.

The core idea is good: user-defined topics + AI generation + multiple delivery formats. The 42 curated topics with semantic search for custom topics is a genuine differentiator. The audio feature with 12 voices is a smart play for commuters. But the product needs to answer one question that it currently doesn't: "Why should I use this instead of asking ChatGPT/Perplexity to summarize today's news?"

The answer should be: scheduling, curation memory, audio delivery, and the topic engine. But those advantages are invisible behind an auth wall. Fix the distribution basics, build a landing page that sells the dream, and this could be a real product. Right now it's a great prototype that nobody can find or pay for.
