## 1. Williams Treaties First Nations take C$700M equity stake in Darlington SMR

- Seven Williams Treaties First Nations communities announced a CAD 700 million ($492 million) investment in Ontario Power Generation's Darlington New Nuclear Project on June 30, 2026 — Canada's largest-ever collective First Nations investment in nuclear power.
- The deal is structured as a loan to Darlington New Nuclear Project LP, funded through the Canada Indigenous Loan Guarantee Corporation, that converts to equity ownership once construction of all four planned BWRX-300 units is complete.
- The seven communities — Alderville, Curve Lake, Hiawatha, Mississaugas of Scugog Island, Beausoleil, Chippewas of Georgina Island, and Chippewas of Rama First Nations — become full equity co-owners in what OPG calls the G7's first small modular reactor project.
- Construction is ongoing, not yet complete: OPG received its Licence to Construct in April 2025, installed the reactor building's 2.1-million-pound basemat foundation in April 2026, and is targeting commercial power from the first BWRX-300 unit in 2029 — despite some aggregator reports this week mischaracterizing the project as already operating.

*Source: [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/first-nations-group-takes-stake-in-ontario-nuclear-plant) · Jun 30, 2026*

## 2. US, Japan and South Korea sign pact to jointly export SMR technology

- Foreign ministers Marco Rubio (US), Motegi Toshimitsu (Japan), and Cho Hyun (South Korea) signed a trilateral Memorandum of Cooperation on July 7, 2026, on the margins of the NATO Summit in Turkey, to jointly accelerate SMR deployments in third countries.
- The framework pairs US reactor technology with Korea's manufacturing and construction capacity and Japan's precision-materials expertise, targeting fleet-style deployments to cut costs and streamline licensing.
- India, Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore and Vietnam are named as initial target markets under the pact's Indo-Pacific focus.
- The US is committing over $10 million in new funding to the State Department's FIRST Program (Foundational Infrastructure for Responsible Use of SMR Technology) to fund project support and an SMR regional training hub; Korean firms Doosan Enerbility, SK Group and HD Hyundai are positioned as likely beneficiaries.

*Source: [The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10810008) · Jul 15, 2026*

## 3. Turkey's IC Holding partners with ARC Clean Technology on 20-reactor SMR venture

- Turkey's IC Holding and US-based ARC Clean Technology announced a venture on July 3, 2026, to deploy up to 20 SMRs across Turkey, the Middle East, Eastern Europe and Central Asia.
- The plan targets up to 10 reactors in Turkey and another 10 across the wider region, using ARC's fourth-generation ARC-100 design — a 100-megawatt sodium-cooled fast reactor priced at roughly $300 million per unit.
- The reactors are pitched for data centers, energy-intensive industry, organized industrial zones, petrochemical facilities, mining sites and desalination plants, particularly in areas with limited grid capacity.
- The venture fits Turkey's broader target of 20 gigawatts of nuclear capacity by 2050, including 5,000 MW to come from SMRs specifically.

*Source: [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/energy/2026/07/turkish-us-venture-to-build-20-small-modular-nuclear-reactors/) · Jul 03, 2026*

## 4. UK developer SGE files plans for 4.2GW, 14-reactor SMR fleet

- UK-based developer SGE submitted plans on July 2, 2026, to build 14 small modular reactors across three UK sites, with a combined capacity of 4.2 gigawatts.
- The company says the fleet's output would be equivalent to about 11% of UK power demand, or enough to supply roughly 8 million homes.
- The filing marks one of the largest single SMR fleet proposals submitted in the UK to date, adding to a growing queue of competing SMR bids as the country works through its nuclear procurement process.

*Source: [BusinessGreen](https://www.businessgreen.com/news/4532120/sge-submits-plans-42gw-uk-small-modular-nuclear-reactor-fleet) · Jul 02, 2026*

## 5. Aalo Atomics microreactor reaches criticality at Idaho National Laboratory

- Aalo Atomics' Critical Test Reactor achieved initial criticality at 00:20 local time on July 4, 2026, at Idaho National Laboratory — the fourth US microreactor to reach criticality under DOE authorization ahead of a self-imposed July 4 deadline, exceeding the original goal of three.
- The full-scale core demonstration is rated at 10 MWe.
- Aalo is expanding into a one-million-square-foot factory to apply assembly-line manufacturing to reactor production, and has begun work on a second reactor, Project Ascension, also rated at 10 MWe, intended to power an on-site data center starting in 2027.
- The company's long-term design packages reactors into modular "Aalo Pods" of up to 50 MWe, purpose-built to co-locate with AI data centers.

*Source: [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/criticality-for-fourth-us-microreactor-meets-deadline) · Jul 04, 2026*
