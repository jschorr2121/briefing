## 1. OpenAI opens GPT-5.6 to the public in three price tiers

- OpenAI publicly launched GPT-5.6 on July 9, 2026, after restricting it to "a small group of trusted partners" since late June.
- The model ships in three variants: Sol, the flagship, at $5/$30 per million input/output tokens; Terra, a mid-tier option at $2.50/$15; and Luna, the budget tier, at $1/$6.
- The wider release came after the Commerce Department's Center for AI Standards and Innovation reviewed the model under President Trump's June executive order requiring 30-day pre-release vetting of powerful AI systems.
- OpenAI paired the launch with ChatGPT Work, a GPT-5.6-powered agent tool built to turn scattered notes and drafts into finished work rather than just answer questions.

## 2. TSMC posts record quarter, pours another $100B into Arizona

- TSMC reported Q2 2026 revenue of $40.2 billion (NT$1.27 trillion), up 36% year-over-year, with net income up 77.4% to NT$706.56 billion — both beating Wall Street estimates.
- Growth was driven by AI-linked demand in the High Performance Computing segment.
- CEO C.C. Wei announced an additional $100 billion commitment to Arizona, bringing total planned investment in the state to $265 billion, aimed at 2-nanometer wafer fabs and advanced packaging — with roughly four more plants possible depending on market conditions.
- TSMC raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $60–64 billion and guided Q3 revenue to $44.6–45.8 billion.

*Source: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tsmc-q2-2026-earnings-record-112109987.html) · Jul 16, 2026*

## 3. Helsing raises $1.8B in Europe's biggest-ever defense-AI round

- German defense-AI company Helsing closed a $1.8 billion Series E on July 13, 2026, at an $18 billion valuation — Europe's largest defense-startup funding round to date.
- The valuation more than doubled from €12 billion in June 2025, with investor demand "significantly" exceeding the available allocation.
- New and existing backers included Lightspeed Venture Partners, General Catalyst, Prima Materia, Accel, and Greenoaks.
- Helsing builds AI software and autonomous systems — including the HX-2 strike drone and Altra battlefield-operations software — and said the funds will accelerate integrating new AI platforms into partner nations' defense capabilities.

## 4. EU unveils action plan to police AI-driven cyber risk

- The European Commission presented its Action Plan on Cybersecurity and Artificial Intelligence on July 7, 2026, to address risks AI poses to digital security while supporting its safe use.
- The plan warns AI "can be misused to identify vulnerabilities, automate attacks and increase the scale and speed of cyber incidents."
- Rather than creating new funding streams, it leans on coordinating existing legal frameworks — the AI Act, Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2 Directive, and DORA — across Member States, industry, and EU bodies.
- Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said the goal is to "harness and focus existing capabilities, networks and the legal framework to fortify the cybersecurity protecting our digital landscape."

*Source: [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-presents-eu-action-plan-cybersecurity-and-artificial-intelligence) · Jul 07, 2026*

## 5. Agility Robotics to go public in first pure-play humanoid-robot listing

- Agility Robotics agreed to go public via a SPAC merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI, announced July 5, 2026, at a roughly $2.5 billion valuation.
- The deal is expected to raise more than $620 million in gross proceeds and would make Agility the first pure-play humanoid robotics company to trade on public markets.
- The Salem, Oregon company — an Oregon State University spinoff founded in 2015 — makes the bipedal Digit robot for warehouse and factory work, with over $300 million in booked multi-year revenue across roughly 1,000 robots.
- CEO Peggy Johnson emphasized execution over hype: "Our biggest competitor right now is just us."
