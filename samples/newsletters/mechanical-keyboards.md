## 1. OpenAI's first hardware product is a keyboard, not a gadget for ChatGPT

- OpenAI and boutique keyboard maker Work Louder launched "Codex Micro" on July 15 — a $230 macro pad built specifically for Codex, OpenAI's coding agent.
- It packs 13 low-profile mechanical keys, a joystick, a rotary encoder, and six programmable layers; six "agent keys" glow different RGB colors to show live Codex thread status (white idle, blue thinking, green complete, amber needs input, red error).
- Body is CNC'd aluminum with polycarbonate parts and legend-less translucent keycaps — it's a limited run, aimed at OpenAI's roughly 5 million weekly Codex users, sold only while supplies last.
- The launch reportedly came weeks after OpenAI's head of Apps told staff not to get "distracted by side quests," which added some extra chatter to the release.

## 2. The KeyBoy Advance turns a Game Boy Advance into a 50% keyboard

- PromoType's KeyBoy Advance, designed by Galo, mimics the GBA's shell — front panel shape, battery-cover-style back edge, a fake power LED, even a speaker-grille detail — around a 50% HHKB-style layout with a 10-key number row.
- It has programmable shoulder buttons standing in for L/R triggers, top-mount construction with O-rings, and a 7-degree typing angle; buyers choose from wired, hot-swap, or full tri-mode (wired/Bluetooth/2.4GHz) PCBs across 9 colorways.
- Pricing starts at $275 and runs to $340 for the wireless polycarbonate version; the group buy closes July 22, 2026, with an estimated Q4 2026 ship date through Prototypist Keyboards and UniKeys.

*Source: [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/07/08/keyboy-advance-game-boy-mechanical-keyboard/) · July 8, 2026*

## 3. Keychron's Orca echo mashes a split keyboard into a trackball and a wheel

- Revealed with Gizmodo Japan and shown first at Computex Taipei (June 2–5), the Orca echo is a 49-key column-staggered split keyboard (Corne-style) with a 19mm trackball under the right thumb, a wheel on the left, and vertical scroll pads on both halves.
- Built-in two-stage tenting stands and a magnetic halves-connect-for-carrying design target ergonomics-focused typists who also want pointer control without leaving home row.
- It runs wired, Bluetooth (3 devices), or 2.4GHz wireless off an 800mAh battery (~240 hours in 1K mode), with keymap customization through Keychron's browser-based Launcher software.
- Crowdfunding opened Japan-first on GIZMART on June 19, 2026.

*Source: [Green Keys](https://green-keys.info/en/keychron-x-gizmodo-announces-the-new-orca-echo-a-split-keyboard-with-a-trackball-and-wheel-launches-in-japan-first-from-june-19/) · June 19, 2026*
